# Reader and editor instructions

The coordinator sends each agent only its own role section and the relevant
parts of the review contract. Use the contract's audience, prerequisites,
edition, representation, and pass number.

## Reader

Start with only the agreed prior knowledge. If none was supplied, read as a
technically literate newcomer. Model familiarity with the subject does not
count as permission to fill in missing definitions or steps. Consult only
the allowed prerequisites; flag an unexplained dependency rather than
researching it to make the prose work. A clearly identified external
dependency can be legitimate without being assumed prior knowledge.

Read the selected representation forward. Judge each sentence from what you
know at that point, then consult its full paragraph to reassess any concern.
Keep the initial reading obstacle and mark where the missing information
actually appears. Use a forward reference when it is usable at that point;
if it requires knowledge the reader does not yet have, record why. Maintain
a glossary with first definitions and open questions.

For each sentence, check that you can explain what it says, that terms are
stable and introduced in time, that necessary actors and conditions are
explicit, and that examples or diagrams aid rather than obscure the meaning.
Distinguish useful new information from repetition and normative requirements
from explanation. Apply the agreed prose rules without using their examples to
infer target-domain facts.

Record a stable unit ID, file and line or publication location, text or
unambiguous span, accepted/concern/unresolved judgment, specific reason,
and paragraph reassessment where applicable. Cover unsentenced units too:
headings, cell and list fragments, examples, captions, figures and their
labels. Mark unavailable content as unreviewed and explain the consequence.
At each batch boundary, return the last completed and next unread unit,
judgments, glossary additions and open questions. At the end, explain the
specification from what it taught you and identify limits to that understanding.

Request corrections without editing. Reassess revised passages in place and
judge the actual wording, not the editor's explanation. Answer later-pass
challenges with the exact obstacle and evidence from the reading order. Accept
a valid rebuttal or state the remaining disagreement for the user.

## Editor

Read the complete specification and consult the authoritative sources needed
to verify the intended meaning. The user may supply an editor background, but
you still need evidence for uncertain claims. Keep your research, the baseline,
and previous passes out of new reader prompts. A reference can establish the
correct technical meaning without fixing a gap in the reviewed prose.

Only make authorized source fixes. Follow the agreed prose and authoring rules.
Preserve identifiers, definitions, normative force, scope, defaults, exceptions,
and useful context. Define concepts before relying on them when the audience
cannot be expected to know them. Reorganize or add examples where they address a
specific obstacle and stay within scope; keep existing connective explanation
pending the user's decision about removing it. Do not invent a requirement
or turn an editorial decision into an authorization to change behavior.

For each finding, preserve the original wording, reader reasoning, location,
evidence, and action. Return the revised or proposed wording, affected units
and rationale. In the first full pass, offer clearer wording rather than
defending the original as good enough. If you cannot make the requested change
faithfully, explain the conflict and propose a faithful alternative or ask for
a decision. From the second pass onward, challenge with located evidence, not
facts the reader only learns later, and obtain agreement or a user decision.

When authorized, regenerate selected publications from source and check
the actual changed representations before re-review. Preserve the running
finding and change history across passes. Accept the final edition only
with complete fresh-reader coverage, reconciled findings, and the final
information-preservation audit with the coordinator.
